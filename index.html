About the Bite Map

A free planning map for inshore anglers around New Zealand and Australia. Pick a spot (or tap any open water) to see the tide curve, the next high/low, live wind, swell and a simple Bite Index. Slide the time bar to scout the next 24 hours.

The UF Bite Index

A transparent 1–10 heuristic that blends six public-data signals: tide movement (28%): moving water beats slack; solunar periods (18%): moon overhead/underfoot "majors" and moonrise/set "minors", amplified near new & full moon; wind (16%): lighter is more fishable; sea state (14%): calm water fishes and launches more easily, while a big swell drags conditions down; pressure trend (12%): a steady fall ahead of a front tends to switch fish on, a sharp post-front rise slows them; and light (12%): the dawn/dusk windows. It is a planning aid, not a proprietary AI model and not a guarantee. Moon and solunar times are computed on-device; pressure is from the forecast model. Local knowledge always wins.

Where the data comes from

Marine conditions (waves, swell, wind & pressure) are supplied by MetService (MetOcean Solutions) where available, falling back to the free Open-Meteo Marine & Weather APIs. Tides, sun & solunar times are Open-Meteo / computed on-device. Satellite imagery: Esri World Imagery. Nautical marks: OpenSeaMap. Base map: OpenStreetMap.

Fish Finder

The Fish Finder shades the map by where a species has actually been recorded, using aggregated occurrence data from GBIF (the Global Biodiversity Information Facility): millions of public records from museum collections, research surveys and citizen-science sightings (e.g. iNaturalist). Records are binned into hexagons and shaded by density: pale = few reports, deep red = many. Because it reflects observation effort as much as the fish (places with more divers, anglers and surveys light up brightest), it’s a guide to a species’ known range and hotspots, not a real-time fish finder or a catch predictor. Use it as a starting point for exploration, then trust the conditions, the structure and local knowledge.

Marine reserves & no-take zones

In New Zealand, the reserve outlines are the official DOC Marine Reserves dataset embedded in this app (lightly simplified for display). In Australia and elsewhere they are community-mapped in OpenStreetMap and are indicative only. Either way this is a planning aid, not the rulebook; it does not include every seasonal closure, mātaitai, or fishing restriction. Always confirm current rules with the official authority before fishing:

  • NZ: DOC marine reserves and Fisheries NZ rules (the NZ Fishing Rules app is handy on the water)
  • AUS: Australian Marine Parks, Great Barrier Reef zoning, plus your state/territory fisheries for in-shore closures

Not for navigation

Model tides are referenced to mean sea level at ~8 km resolution and can differ from official chart datums, especially inside harbours and estuaries. For navigation-grade tide tables use the official sources:

  • NZ: LINZ tide predictions & NIWA Tide Forecaster
  • AUS: Bureau of Meteorology tide predictions
